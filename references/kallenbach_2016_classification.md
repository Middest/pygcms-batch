# Kallenbach 2016 Classification Scheme

Reference: Kallenbach et al. 2016, "Direct evidence for microbial-derived soil organic matter formation and its ecophysiological controls", Nature Communications 7:13630. DOI: 10.1038/ncomms13630

## 8 Origin-Based Classes

| Class | Description | Typical compounds |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|
| Lipids | Fatty acids, alkanes, alkenes, sterols, terpenes | n-alkanes, fatty acids, wax esters, cholesterol derivatives |
| Lignin derivatives | Lignin monomers and methoxyphenols | Guaiacyl, syringyl, vanillin, coniferyl/sinapyl derivatives |
| Polysaccharides | Sugar derivatives, furans, pyrans, chitin markers | Levoglucosan, furfural, anhydro-sugars, acetylfuran |
| Proteins | N-bearing from amino acid/protein pyrolysis | Nitriles, pyridines, pyrroles, pyrazines, amines, amides |
| Non-protein N-bearing | Other N-containing compounds | Nitro, nitroso, urea, guanidine, nucleobases, ammonium |
| Phenolics | Phenolic compounds | Phenol, cresol, catechol, guaiacol, syringol, vanillic acids |
| Aromatics | Aromatic hydrocarbons (non-phenolic) | Benzene, toluene, xylene, naphthalene, PAHs |
| Unspecified | Multiple origins, halogen/Si/P/S-containing | Chlorinated compounds, siloxanes, unknowns, complex mixtures |

## Source Attribution

Kallenbach classifies compounds primarily by biochemical origin:

### Plant-derived
- Lignin derivatives
- Phenolics (as plant structural components)
- Long-chain lipids (>20C, plant waxes)

### Microbial-derived
- Proteins (N-bearing from microbial biomass)
- Aromatics (from microbial metabolism)
- Short-chain lipids (<16C, microbial membrane)
- Chitin markers (fungal cell walls)

### Mixed / Unspecified
- Polysaccharides (can be plant cellulose or microbial EPS)
- Non-protein N-bearing (diverse origins)
- Unspecified compounds

## Key Difference from Chen 2023

Kallenbach uses fewer, broader classes focused on **biochemical origin** rather than detailed chemical structure. The key insight from Kallenbach is that microbial processing of simple substrates can produce chemically diverse, stable SOM — challenging the paradigm that SOM chemical diversity requires plant-derived inputs.
